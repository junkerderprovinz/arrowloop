Every button has a symbol of its own, the desktop app draws its title bar in the look you chose, and the welcome page shows the way to the first sync as a small scene. On the phone, a job that reaches a server waits for the network instead of failing, and OpenCloud targets get past files that differ only in their time.

## ✨ Added

- **The desktop app draws its own title bar on Windows and Linux.** It takes the colours, corners and accent of the look you chose and holds only Minimise, Maximise and Close, since the sidebar shows the logo and the name. Drag it to move the window, double-click it to maximise and pull any edge to resize. It stays clear while a dialog is open, and Close still hides to the notification area or quits as before. macOS keeps its own title bar.

## 🎨 Design

- **Every button has a symbol of its own,** so Settings › Look › Labels › Symbol leaves no word among the symbols. The answers to a conflict, Check for updates and its answer, and New in Settings › Info have one, on the phone too. Buttons that borrowed another meaning's symbol get their own: Import and Export, logging in with a password or a passkey, Forgot password, Done, Use this folder, Send a test, putting a file back, emptying and clearing out the trash, stopping a run, the password and two-factor authentication. Open in the tray menu shows the app's own mark.
- **Every choice under Settings › Look › Labels shows a symbol before its name,** as Design, Corners and Motion do. On a phone, and in a browser narrower than 600 pixels, these choices stand two to a row, so their names keep their words.
- **The welcome page shows the way to the first sync as a small scene.** Each step card has a picture, and a pointer walks through all three: it adds a target, sets up a job and runs the first sync, which ends in a badge and a burst of confetti. The three step numbers share the accent and pop in one after another, and a step done while the page is open draws a ring before its number turns into a tick. The whole card leads to its step, not only the title. The scene follows the Motion setting and your system's reduced motion.
- **The trash wears a bin,** in the sidebar, in a job's menu and on the phone.
- **The F-Droid button in Settings › Apps shows F-Droid's own logo in its colours.**
- **The two sides of a new job are called Device folder and Target folder.** They read On the device and A target before.
- **The schedule bar on the phone spans its card.** In a job and in the global sync settings, Manual, Every N, Every day, Weekdays, Real time and Cron stand under the Runs label across the whole card.
- **ArrowLoop follows GlimStone 3.8.0,** the design language these symbols come from.

## ⚡ Improved

- **Version numbers have no v,** 2.4.0 rather than v2.4.0, in Settings › Info, on the cards in Settings › Apps and in the messages about an update. Each number still opens its release.
- **A running job's progress bar costs the page less,** since nothing around it is laid out again at each step.
- **The engine and the desktop app are built with Go 1.27.2** and golang.org/x/net 0.62.0, which bring the latest fixes of the standard library and the network package.

## 🐛 Fixed

- **A job that reaches a server waits while the phone has no network.** A battery saver that turns wifi off at night made a nightly job fail three times, then wait for the next night, which was offline again. The job now stays due and runs with the first wake-up that has a network. Jobs between two folders on the phone run as before.
- **OpenCloud targets get past a file that differs only in its time.** OpenCloud accepts a new modification time and keeps the old one, so two files of the same size never matched and every run of the job stopped on them. ArrowLoop sends such a file again in full.
- **Check this job on the phone shows what it found.** The button asked the engine and showed nothing. The findings are listed under the buttons, and Check this job in a job card's menu opens the job and checks it there.
- **A preview that fails on the phone names the job and the reason.** It said the engine could not be reached even when the engine had answered.
- **A side without a trash folder no longer fills the engine log with errors.** Every run logged a missing trash as an error, although a side that never lost a file has none.

## 🔒 Security

- **Marking a drive accepts only a drive ArrowLoop offers.** The request could name any folder the engine can write to and left a marker file there, which no job would ever find. A folder that is not one of the offered drives is refused.
- **Dependency updates close five open advisories.** The web interface's test tooling takes undici 8.12.3, the phone app takes zod 4.6.6 and node-forge 1.4.2, and the engine and the desktop app take chi 5.4.0.
