The sign-in is new, with sign-in services such as Pocket ID, sessions that last through restarts and API tokens for dashboards and scripts. The phone gets the built-in VPN, which can leave out the wifi you trust, automation apps can start a job, and targets take any name.

## ✨ Added

- **Sign in through a sign-in service.** Under **Settings**, **Security**, **Sign-in services** you can add Pocket ID, authentik, Authelia, Keycloak, Kanidm, Zitadel or any other OpenID Connect service. Enter its address, client ID and secret, run the test sign-in, and from then on the sign-in page has a button for it. Who may come in is decided at the service.
- **API tokens.** A token that reads the status and log is all a dashboard such as Homepage or Homarr needs, and a token for everything opens the whole API for a script. Each one is shown once and lasts 90 days unless you choose otherwise. Your read tokens keep working as tokens that read the status and log.
- **Sessions you can see.** The **Sessions** card lists every browser that is signed in, and you can sign any of them out, or all the others at once.
- **The password check on changes.** Before a new passkey, token or sign-in service, and before showing the pairing phrase, ArrowLoop asks for your password when you signed in more than ten minutes ago, instead of signing you out. You can turn the question off.
- **`ARROWLOOP_PUBLIC_URL`.** The address you open ArrowLoop at through your reverse proxy, such as `https://arrowloop.example.com`. Passkeys and sign-in services need it, and the Unraid template has a field for it.
- **Start a job from Tasker or MacroDroid.** ArrowLoop is a Locale plug-in action: pick it in your automation app, then one job or All jobs. The run counts as one you started yourself and lands in History, and a start that cannot go through notifies you like a failed run. Each action carries a proof signed with a key only this install holds, so another app can at most repeat a choice you made. From Android 12 on, background starts go straight through only while battery optimisation is off for ArrowLoop. Thanks to @CN-Cai for the idea and the detailed request ([#267](https://github.com/junkerderprovinz/arrowloop/issues/267)).
- **VPN in the Android app.** Settings › Integrations sets up a VPN from your provider or from your own WireGuard config, and a job picks the tunnel it goes through, as on a server or the desktop app. The tunnel runs inside ArrowLoop, so it needs no root and no Android VPN slot.
- **No VPN on your own wifi.** On the phone, a VPN can leave out the wifi networks you trust, such as the one at home. Open **Wifi without VPN** from the shield button in its row, type a name or tap **Add the current wifi**, and runs on those networks connect directly. Android names the wifi only to apps with location access, so ArrowLoop asks for it with the first network, and timed runs while the app is closed need it all the time. Before a run goes direct, the engine checks that its traffic leaves by that wifi, so a phone that just left home stays on the VPN. Thanks to Andreas for the idea.
- **Update the desktop app from the Version card.** Once Check for updates has found a newer release, Update now fetches it at once and Restart opens the new version. The installed copy on Windows asks for administrator rights first.
- **Timestamp tolerance on the phone.** A job on the phone can set its own timestamp tolerance, like any other value it can take from the global sync settings.
- **Two-factor sign-in for Internxt.** Adding an Internxt target runs its sign-in and asks for the code when the account has two-factor login on. Thanks to @Zeitsperre for the report ([#257](https://github.com/junkerderprovinz/arrowloop/issues/257)).

## 🎨 Design

- **The sign-in page and the Security tile come from GlimStone 4.9.0.** The page has at most two buttons, **Sign in** and the sign-in service when one is on. Forgot password? is a plain link below them, a new password under twelve characters gets a quiet hint that a longer one is harder to guess, and your browser offers a saved passkey right in the password field.
- **Version, Components, Help and About.** The Info tile shows the app with its number, the update switch and where the updates stand in the Version card, and rclone, GlimStone and the installer move to the Components card. The card says "Not checked yet" until the first check, and the phone names the store it came from.
- **Target and folder, side by side.** The job form shows the target and the folder on it as two fields. Job cards, the folder picker and History show a side as the target's name and the folder, such as "Home Lab - Keller: home/a".
- **Notifications have a tile of their own.** The channels moved out of Safety net into their own settings tile. Thanks to @danpeig for the idea ([#254](https://github.com/junkerderprovinz/arrowloop/issues/254)).
- **Every VPN provider has its logo.** The tiles under Settings, Integrations and the rows of your VPNs draw each provider's own mark, and the Test, Edit and Remove buttons of a connected service use the same glyphs everywhere.
- **Tidier connection rows.** Buttons stand a step off the row, badges are a size smaller and move under a long name before it is cut, and wordmarks start where a symbol would.
- **Small touches on the phone.** The screen of your targets and the overview's card are called Targets, as in the menus, an empty trash shows no size, the overview leaves adding a job to the Jobs page, and the VPN line under a job turns into a warning only where its runs would fail. Thanks to @danpeig for the list ([#264](https://github.com/junkerderprovinz/arrowloop/issues/264)).

## ⚡ Improved

- **Staying signed in.** A sign-in lasts 30 days, or 7 without a visit, and an update or a restart no longer signs you out.
- **Your password, two-factor authentication and passkeys move along.** On the first start, ArrowLoop takes over what it kept in `security.json` and stores it in `signin.json`. Nobody has to sign in again or set anything up again, apart from the one sign-in a restart always cost.
- **Everything works without a password.** An install without a password answers on any host name, so a reverse proxy or a name of your own works straight away, and every route is open, from the settings backup and restore to emptying the trash for good. The Security tile says that anyone who can reach the address can then do everything, and a password puts all of it behind the sign-in again. Thanks to @danpeig for raising it ([#266](https://github.com/junkerderprovinz/arrowloop/issues/266)).
- **Any password length.** Setting or resetting a password takes any length. Below twelve characters the form suggests a longer one, but the choice is yours.
- **Ten recovery codes.** New recovery codes come as ten instead of eight. The ones you have keep working until you make new ones.
- **A stranger can no longer lock you out.** Wrong passwords make the next try wait a little longer instead of locking the address for a minute.
- **`ARROWLOOP_PASSWORD_HASH` still wins.** The password from the environment still beats the one set in the interface, and the sign-in page leaves out Forgot password? while it is set. A new hash signs out every browser at the next start.
- **`ARROWLOOP_HOSTS` can go.** It listed the names an install without a password answered on, and with that check gone it does nothing. You can remove it from your template or compose file.
- **Other fortress apps open from here without a password too.** The tab belongs to the browser that opened it, and the other app still asks for its own sign-in. When an older app turns down Open, the message asks for an update of that app instead of a password.
- **Name a target what you like, and rename it later.** Spaces, dashes, umlauts and emoji are all fine, so "OneDrive - Business" or "SFTP Home Lab" work as they are. Only an empty name is refused, and two targets can't share one. Behind the name every target keeps a fixed ID, which the jobs, the folder picker and rclone use, so a rename changes nothing in your jobs. Existing targets keep their old name as the ID, and older backups restore as before. Thanks to @danpeig for the request and for testing it so thoroughly on the phone ([#271](https://github.com/junkerderprovinz/arrowloop/issues/271)).
- **Less memory in the tray.** A copy started straight into the tray frees what the window used, and the tray icon only spins while a job runs. On Windows the app drops to a few megabytes once no window is open. Thanks to @danpeig for the careful measurements ([#256](https://github.com/junkerderprovinz/arrowloop/issues/256)).
- **A job's database is named after its id.** New jobs keep their records in `state/<id>.db`, and existing ones are renamed once on start, so renaming a job leaves no old name behind. Thanks to @danpeig for the suggestion ([#252](https://github.com/junkerderprovinz/arrowloop/issues/252)).
- **Run notes read as whole sentences.** Every note in a run's details is a full sentence, and a file put off as written while copied names what changed, such as its time before and after. A file that grows after its last read during a local copy counts as written during the copy too, instead of as a failed copy.
- **Dashboard labels.** Dashboards that read container labels list ArrowLoop with its name, logo and description on their own. Put the address in the template's Dashboard Link field to make the entry a link. The Unraid template and compose.yaml carry the same labels and the API token field.
- **Tighter checks on paths and addresses.** A state path in the configuration is held to the state folder, and the Linux desktop app reads memory map addresses at the width of the platform.
- **Weekly OS updates for the image.** The published container image picks up the base system's updates every week, between releases too.
- **The privacy policy covers the VPN.** It says what `vpn.json` keeps (your login, encrypted with this install's key, and the wifi networks without VPN), which providers ArrowLoop signs in to, and that the location access only ever reads the wifi's name. None of it reaches us.

## 🐛 Fixed

- **Signing out also ends the live updates.** A page left open after its sign-in ended, or after its token was revoked, kept getting job progress until it reloaded. It stops within a few seconds now.
- **The privacy policy covers sign-in services.** It says what ArrowLoop learns from a sign-in service and keeps in `signin.json`.
- **Two-way jobs onto a WebDAV share that keeps no file times.** Stalwart and similar servers give every file its upload time and keep no checksum. ArrowLoop took that time for a change and never recorded the files as synced, so each later run wanted to overwrite all of them and the change brake held the job. It now ignores the time where a side keeps none and compares the bytes instead. A stuck job settles on its next run without sending anything, and a first run onto a share with the same files no longer turns each into a conflict. Thanks to @speed47 for the report and the exact steps ([#274](https://github.com/junkerderprovinz/arrowloop/issues/274)).
- **A share that keeps old times no longer stops a job.** When one side has no checksum, as on an SMB share, and a file's times are far apart while its size matches, ArrowLoop compares both files byte by byte before refusing the pair. The same content is recorded and the job runs on, different content still stops the run. Thanks to @maxtesterr for the detailed report ([#260](https://github.com/junkerderprovinz/arrowloop/issues/260)).
- **Copies to MEGA are recorded.** MEGA keeps neither a file's modification time nor a checksum, so after an upload the time on MEGA is the moment of the upload. ArrowLoop put every finished copy off as written during the copy, until the job reported an error. On a target without times it compares the size, as rclone does, and a job stuck this way settles after one more run. Thanks to sipsip6770 on Discord for the very careful report ([#251](https://github.com/junkerderprovinz/arrowloop/issues/251)).
- **SMB shares through the VPN.** A job whose side is an SMB share, such as the NAS on a FritzBox, refused to run through the built-in VPN. ArrowLoop passes each SMB connection through the tunnel and only through it, so the share is not reached at all while the VPN is down. The Targets page, the target form and the folder picker also go through the VPN of the job on a target. An SMB target that signs in with Kerberos still cannot use the VPN. Thanks to a beta tester for the report.
- **WireGuard on Android.** A valid config failed its test on the phone and no packet left it, because WireGuard asked Android for a socket it does not give apps. The tunnel comes up without it, a device that still refuses gives its own reason, and a .conf saved with a byte order mark reads as well.
- **A saved WireGuard config shows that it is saved.** The config field came back empty, which looked as if nothing had been stored. It reads "Saved", on the web and on the phone.
- **No failure notices from a phone that was offline.** With wifi and mobile data off, for example in flight mode overnight, ArrowLoop holds the jobs that need a server and runs them at the first wake-up with a network. A run that loses the network partway counts as held, and the rest follows at the next wake-up instead of the job's next turn. Thanks to @kdqic3 for the clear description ([#262](https://github.com/junkerderprovinz/arrowloop/issues/262)).
- **ArrowLoop on Chromebooks from Google Play.** The Play version had the engine only for ARM, which Chromebooks with an Intel or AMD processor cannot run. It carries the x86_64 engine too, the same one the x86_64 APK has. A test runs the database under the system call rules Android sets for apps on x86_64, so a library update that brings back a forbidden call fails the build instead of the app.
- **The phone finds its microSD card.** The folder picker lists cards and USB drives under the name Android gives them, and leaves out "/", which Android does not let an app open. A refused folder says why instead of showing "HTTP 400".
- **Targets on the phone behave.** Save always answers, with the reason under the name field and next to the buttons when the engine refuses a name. A target on a phone folder stays in Targets after saving, and a rename changes the target you edited instead of leaving a copy without its passwords.
- **The phone's forms and History keep up.** A target you connect under More, Targets while the job form is open can be chosen right away, and History shows a new run by itself, also one that ran while the app was in the background.
- **History says which way a file went.** On the phone a copied file reads "Phone/Photos → NAS/Photos" with the names the job card uses, a file sent to the trash says on which side, and a renamed job keeps its rows. On the web a deleted or renamed file names only its side, and a moved file's arrow points the right way. Thanks to @danpeig for the report ([#250](https://github.com/junkerderprovinz/arrowloop/issues/250)).
- **A safer WebSocket library.** The notification channel for MQTT brings in gorilla/websocket, which made the mask of each outgoing WebSocket frame with a weak random generator (GO-2026-6278). ArrowLoop ships the fixed version in the container, the desktop app and the phone's engine.
- **AppImage on the download page.** The docs' download section lists the AppImages for x64 and ARM. Thanks to @danpeig ([#255](https://github.com/junkerderprovinz/arrowloop/issues/255)).
- **Badges keep their descenders.** A long label in a run's file list no longer loses the bottom of letters such as g and p.

## 🌐 Translations

- **Run notes in every language.** The notes in a run's details are translated in the web app and on the phone, with numbers and times in your locale. The red message above a job's runs names its files in your language too, and so does a sign-in refused when a target is saved.
- **New words in all 42 languages.** The sign-in and the Security tile, the automation picker and its failure notice, the WireGuard start message, the Wifi without VPN screen with its notes about location access, and the messages on a target's name field.
